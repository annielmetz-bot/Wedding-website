Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before you pack your bags.

September is one of the best months to visit northern New Mexico. Expect warm, sunny days (highs in the low-to-mid 70s°F) and cool nights (can drop into the 40s°F). The summer monsoon season usually wraps up by mid-September, so you may see some afternoon thunderstorms early in the month, but they're typically brief and dramatic.

The Sangre de Cristo Mountains are stunning in September, and the aspens may start turning golden — it can be spectacular. Come prepared for warm afternoons and chilly evenings.

The wedding is outdoors at a property in Arroyo Seco. The ceremony and reception will be on the grounds, so you'll be walking on grass and dirt paths. Please skip the stilettos — block heels, wedges, or flats are your friends here.

For the ceremony, think "festive outdoor wedding in the desert mountains." We love bold colors, florals, prints, and anything that feels celebratory and Southwestern-inspired. More details on dress code will be on your invitation.

Bring a layer for the evening — once the sun goes down, it cools off quickly at this elevation. A light jacket or shawl is a good call.

Ceremony start time and full schedule details will be on your invitation. In general, plan to arrive at the venue 20–30 minutes before the ceremony start time. The venue address is 48 Rim Road, Arroyo Seco, NM.

Coming from downtown Taos, allow about 15 minutes for the drive. From Arroyo Seco village, it's just a few minutes.

Taos sits at about 6,900 feet and Arroyo Seco at around 7,500 feet. If you're coming from sea level or close to it, altitude can affect you more than you expect — even if you're in great shape. Common symptoms include headache, fatigue, shortness of breath, and feeling tipsy faster than usual.

Our tips:

  • Arrive a day or two early if you can, to acclimate before the wedding.
  • Drink lots of water — way more than you think you need.
  • Go easy on alcohol the first day or two (it hits harder at altitude).
  • Avoid strenuous hiking on your first day.
  • Ibuprofen or acetaminophen can help with altitude headaches.

Rideshare in Taos is very limited — Uber and Lyft exist but are not reliable. Don't count on them for getting home after the reception.

Taos has meant something to us since our first road trip through New Mexico together. The landscape is dramatic and alive — the mountains, the high desert plateau, the deep Rio Grande Gorge, the light at golden hour. It's a place that draws artists and seekers and people who want to feel small in the best possible way.

Arroyo Seco sits at the base of the Sangre de Cristo mountains, just below Taos Ski Valley. It's quiet and beautiful and exactly the kind of place we wanted for this. We can't wait to share it with you.
