Make the most of your trip

Things to Do

Taos has been drawing artists, adventurers, and seekers for over a century. Here's our guide to why.

🎨

Art & Culture

Must Do

Taos Pueblo

North of Taos Plaza · taospueblo.com

A living Native American community and UNESCO World Heritage Site, continuously inhabited for over 1,000 years. Tours are available daily (check website for hours and closures). One of the most significant historic sites in North America. Don't miss it.

Art

Taos Art Museum at Fechin House

Ledoux Street · taosartmuseum.org

A gorgeous historic home and studio built by Russian artist Nicolai Fechin, showcasing the work of the Taos Society of Artists. Beautiful carved woodwork throughout the house. A gem even if you're not usually a museum person.

Art

Harwood Museum of Art

Ledoux Street · harwoodmuseum.org

The second oldest art museum in New Mexico, featuring a fantastic collection of Taos artists from the late 1800s to today. The Agnes Martin gallery alone is worth the trip — seven huge minimalist canvases in a hushed, serene room.

Culture

San Francisco de Asís Church

Ranchos de Taos · ~4 miles south

One of the most photographed buildings in America — and for good reason. The massive adobe buttresses of this 18th-century Spanish Colonial church are iconic. Georgia O'Keeffe and Ansel Adams both captured it. Visit in the early morning light.

Art

Millicent Rogers Museum

North of Taos · millicentrogers.org

An outstanding collection of Native American and Spanish Colonial art, jewelry, pottery, and textiles — largely assembled by style icon Millicent Rogers in the 1940s. Stunning collection, beautiful museum building with mountain views.

Art

Galleries on the Plaza & Ledoux St.

Downtown Taos

Taos has a thriving gallery scene — just wander. Ledoux Street is lined with them. You'll find traditional New Mexican landscapes alongside contemporary work, jewelry, ceramics, and textiles. Most are free and open daily.

🥾

Hiking

Altitude reminder

Many hikes start above 8,000 feet. If you're coming from lower elevations, start with shorter trails and drink lots of water. September is beautiful — warm days, cool nights, and the aspens may be starting to turn gold.

Scenic

Rio Grande Gorge

West of Taos · ~10 miles

Drive out to the Rio Grande Gorge Bridge (the third-highest suspension bridge in the US) and walk across for stunning views into the 800-foot-deep gorge. From there, hike the rim trail in either direction for sweeping views of the gorge and the plateau.

Easy–Moderate

Williams Lake Trail

Taos Ski Valley · ~4 miles round-trip

A beautiful hike up to a gorgeous alpine lake below Wheeler Peak — the highest point in New Mexico. Starts at ~10,000 feet, so you'll want to be acclimated. Bring layers; it can be cold even in September. Stunning views.

Easy

Arroyo Seco Area Walks

Near the wedding venue

The area around Arroyo Seco is gorgeous for casual walks — high desert scrub with views of the Sangre de Cristo range. No formal trails needed; just walk out toward the mountains. Perfect for an early morning stroll the day of the wedding.

Hot Springs

Manby Hot Springs

West of Taos · moderate hike to springs

Natural hot springs in the Rio Grande Gorge. Access requires a 1.5-mile hike down into the gorge (and back up). The springs are clothing-optional, and the setting — sitting in a hot pool in the gorge with the river rushing past — is incredible.

🛍️

Shopping

Shopping

Taos Plaza

Downtown Taos

The historic central plaza is surrounded by shops, galleries, and restaurants. Wander the portal (covered walkway) shops for turquoise jewelry, weavings, santos, and other New Mexican crafts. Mix of tourist traps and genuine gems — take your time.

Shopping

Arroyo Seco Village

Arroyo Seco · near the venue

The tiny village of Arroyo Seco (near the wedding!) has a lovely cluster of shops — the Taos Cow ice cream shop, a great general store, a few galleries, and some of our favorite places to browse. A perfect stop before or after the wedding.

Shopping

Overland Sheepskin Co.

Taos

Famous for their beautiful sheepskin and leather coats, hats, and accessories. Even if you don't buy anything, it's a fun stop. You'll leave wanting a sheepskin jacket. (You'll probably buy one.)

🗺️

Day Trips

Day Trip

Santa Fe

~1.5 hours south

New Mexico's capital and cultural hub. World-class museums (Georgia O'Keeffe Museum, New Mexico Museum of Art), the famous Canyon Road gallery district, incredible food, and a beautiful historic plaza. A perfect day trip if you're staying an extra day.

Day Trip

Enchanted Circle Scenic Byway

84-mile loop from Taos

A stunning 84-mile scenic drive through the Sangre de Cristo Mountains passing through Eagle Nest, Angel Fire, and Red River. Great fall color in September if the aspens are turning. Pack a picnic and take your time on this one.

Day Trip

High Road to Taos

Between Taos and Santa Fe

The scenic mountain route between Taos and Santa Fe passes through tiny Hispanic villages, ancient churches, and gorgeous high desert landscapes. Chimayó (home of the famous Santuario de Chimayó) is a highlight. Perfect if you're driving between the two cities.
